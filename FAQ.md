# Frequently Asked Questions

Anticipated critiques, honest responses. This document is not a defense — it is a thinking companion. If the responses are unsatisfying, improve them.

## On the framework itself

### Isn't this just another utopian manifesto?

Maybe. The test is whether the mechanisms are buildable and whether they address real coordination failures. Every element in the framework has a working precedent: DAO governance, Bitcoin settlement, decentralized identity, quadratic voting, multi-sig treasuries, zero-knowledge proofs. The novelty is synthesis at planetary scale, not invention of new primitives.

If you think the synthesis fails, show where. That critique strengthens the commons.

### Who would actually join?

Early: crypto-native professionals, DAO builders, Bitcoin maximalists, solarpunk organizers, disillusioned NGO workers, people in countries with collapsed monetary systems, people under capital controls, people whose governments are failing them in concrete ways.

Scaling: anyone who finds the sat-denominated economy more useful than the fiat-denominated one. Which becomes a larger population every year.

It does not need universal adoption to matter. It needs enough adoption to create working alternatives.

### This sounds like a cult.

Cults have leaders. This has none. Cults require obedience. This invites forking. Cults punish dissent. This documents expect criticism and provides mechanisms for it. Cults are closed. This is CC0.

If a cult forms around a fork of this framework, that fork has failed. Do not join it.

### Why Bitcoin specifically? Isn't it too volatile / slow / environmental?

The framework treats Bitcoin as the settlement layer, not the daily transaction layer. Lightning and other Layer 2 systems handle speed and cost. Volatility diminishes as adoption grows. Environmental concerns have been overstated (Bitcoin mining is increasingly renewable-powered and acts as a flexible load balancer for grids); the energy use is the cost of decentralization and security that no proof-of-stake system has matched.

More importantly: Bitcoin is the only asset in human history that is simultaneously scarce, neutral, borderless, transparent, and ungoverned. That combination is the requirement. If a better option emerges, the framework can adapt — that is what self-amendment is for.

### Why not a new token designed for this purpose?

Because the moment the framework issues its own settlement token:
- Someone controls the issuance
- The token's value becomes tied to the framework's survival (conflict of interest)
- Speculation attacks the governance layer
- Early insiders capture disproportionate upside
- The entire project becomes a cryptocurrency project rather than a coordination protocol

Bitcoin is already solved money. The framework is for what money does not solve.

### Why reputation and not democracy?

The framework is democratic — but informed democracy, not mob rule. One-person-one-vote systems are vulnerable to low-information voting, sybil attacks, and tyranny of uninformed majorities. Reputation-weighted voting ensures that influence on any given decision correlates with demonstrated understanding of the domain.

But reputation alone could entrench elites. Quadratic voting counters that. Delegated proof-of-stake lets participants borrow expertise without surrendering sovereignty. Reputation decay prevents ossification. The mechanisms are designed to balance.

Is it perfect? No. Is it better than the alternatives currently governing you? That's the question.

### What stops the reputation system from becoming a social credit system?

Reputation in this framework is:
- Tied to verified contributions, not beliefs or behavior
- Earned through peer validation, not algorithmic surveillance
- Non-transferable, so it cannot be traded or coerced
- Decays naturally, so inactivity reduces it
- Cannot be reduced except through community consensus with appeal rights

A social credit system punishes people for what they say or believe. This reputation system recognizes people for what they build. These are different mechanisms with different failure modes.

The risk of abuse exists. That is why transparency is mandatory and why the framework is self-amending — if the reputation system is corrupted, participants can fork.

### Who decides what a "good contribution" is?

Working groups in their domain. Peer validation at the micro level. Community voting at the macro level. Disputes escalate through a structured process with appeal rights.

No single arbiter. No unreviewable authority. All decisions recorded on-chain and subject to challenge.

## On the Great Conversion

### Won't the wealthy just keep their wealth?

Yes. Model C of the Great Conversion preserves relative wealth by design. Prior fiat holdings convert to sats at market rate. A billionaire becomes a sat-billionaire.

This is a feature, not a bug. Any system that requires expropriation to function cannot be adopted voluntarily, which means it cannot be adopted without coercion, which the framework forbids.

What changes: poverty is abolished at the floor through the Universal Basic Endowment. The distance between bottom and top is preserved; the bottom rises.

### A 10% pledge from voluntary participants can't fund a Universal Basic Endowment for everyone.

True, at the beginning. The UBE starts small and grows with participation. Early participants receive meaningful endowments because the participant base is small. As participation grows, the endowment-per-participant may initially drop — but the framework's treasury, pledges, retroactive public goods funding, and contribution rewards compound over time.

Bitcoin's appreciation does the heavy lifting. A UBE denominated in sats grows in real purchasing power as Bitcoin monetizes. A small number of sats today may be a meaningful endowment decades hence.

The UBE is not a substitute for existing social safety nets. It is a complementary floor that persists when those safety nets fail — which, for most humans on Earth, they already do.

### Governments will shut this down.

Some will try. Most will not be able to.

The framework does not require any government's permission to exist. It runs on public blockchain infrastructure. Its identity system is self-sovereign. Its treasury is multi-sig across jurisdictions. Its participants are anonymous by default.

Governments can block specific exchanges or ban specific activities within their borders — but they cannot ban mathematics, and they cannot ban the global internet without consequences their populations will not accept.

Jurisdictions that suppress the framework will lose talent and capital to those that don't. The competitive pressure is real. Small jurisdictions (Switzerland, Singapore, El Salvador, Wyoming, Estonia) already compete for this kind of activity.

### What about taxes?

Participants remain subject to the laws of jurisdictions they live in. The framework does not counsel tax evasion. Taxes on sat-denominated income and capital gains are the participant's responsibility under their local law.

Over time, as the framework demonstrates superior public goods provision, participants may petition their governments to recognize sat-denominated transactions for tax purposes — or emigrate to jurisdictions that already do.

### What prevents a whale from gaming the snapshot?

The Standardized Conversion Rate is a volume-weighted average across the 30 days preceding the snapshot. Single-day manipulation is impossible. Sustained manipulation across 30 days would require capital deployment at a scale that distorts the actual Bitcoin market — at which point the "manipulation" has become the market.

Additional protections: participant holdings are self-attested with zero-knowledge proofs, so a whale cannot claim a position they do not actually hold. Sybil resistance on UBE prevents a whale from multiplying their floor allocation.

## On the distribution

### Why anonymous?

Because every previous attempt at world government has collapsed under the weight of its founders' egos, the institutional ossification of its foundations, or the concentration of power that comes with celebrity leadership.

The framework cannot be co-opted by its creator because it has no creator to co-opt. It cannot be sold by its owner because it has no owner.

Bitcoin is the precedent. Satoshi Nakamoto disappeared. The protocol survived. The framework aspires to the same property.

### How do I know this isn't a scam?

Because there is nothing to buy.

There is no token, no ICO, no premine, no presale, no foundation soliciting donations, no paid membership, no exclusive access, no upsell, no product, no service, no coin, no NFT, no DAO equity, no SAFT.

If anyone asks you for money in the name of this framework, they are scamming you. Ignore them.

### Who benefits from this being released?

Everyone who participates. No one disproportionately. That is the design.

If the framework benefits you more than it benefits others, you have misunderstood it — or someone has corrupted the fork you are using.

### Is this AI-generated?

Probably partly. The synthesis of ideas from DAO governance, Bitcoin theory, and decentralized identity research is the kind of work AI-assisted writing accelerates.

Does that matter? The ideas stand or fall on their own logic, regardless of their provenance. The test is not "who wrote it" but "does it work."

Fork it. Rewrite it in your own voice if that feels better. The ideas belong to humanity.

## On participation

### How do I actually join?

There is no central registry to join. There is no application form. There is no "membership."

You participate by:
- Reading the framework and understanding it
- Building the infrastructure it describes
- Starting or joining a working group around a cause you care about
- Contributing to existing DAOs that share the framework's principles
- Using Bitcoin for your own financial sovereignty
- Translating, forking, critiquing, and propagating the document

Participation is a practice, not a subscription.

### I'm not technical. Can I still contribute?

Yes. The framework needs:
- Translators
- Writers explaining the ideas for new audiences
- Organizers starting working groups
- Designers making the documentation accessible
- Teachers running workshops
- Community builders
- Critics strengthening the ideas through rigorous dissent
- Printers producing physical copies
- People in regions the ideas haven't reached

Technical skill is one contribution channel. It is not the only one.

### What if I disagree with parts of it?

Fork it. Change the parts you disagree with. Publish your version. If it is better, people will use it. If it is worse, they won't. Either way, the commons grows.

Dissent is contribution.

### Where do I start?

Read SEED.md. Pin the document to IPFS. Translate a paragraph. Share it with one person. Build one thing.

The next version is yours to write.
